# Product vision — Confidentiality and know-how protection in OpenAI use

## Opportunity

AI assistance offers businesses opportunities to analyse information, develop
software and explore ideas. Using it can also involve sharing customer data,
source code, commercial plans and unpublished research with external services.
Organizations need a clear basis for deciding when that exchange is acceptable
and how to protect the information that gives them value.

## Problem

Business and technical decision makers need to understand what happens to
information submitted to AI services, what protection they can reasonably
expect, and which responsibilities remain with their organization.

Without a shared understanding, employees may disclose sensitive material,
restrictions may prevent useful work, and decision makers may place more
confidence in contractual promises or technical safeguards than those measures
justify.

## Intended readers

The guide is intended for business owners, technology leaders, security teams
and legal advisers responsible for evaluating or governing organizational use
of OpenAI services.

They need to connect the value of a proposed use with the sensitivity of its
information, the conditions of external processing, the available safeguards
and the organization's willingness to accept the remaining risk.

The guide should serve three organizational contexts:

- Small organizations, where individuals may combine business, technology and
  security responsibilities and have limited specialist support.
- Medium-sized organizations, which need coordinated rules and responsibilities
  across teams while working within constrained budgets and operating capacity.
- Large organizations, which need to coordinate adoption across business units,
  specialist functions and complex information environments.

Each should be able to identify an approach proportionate to its needs and
resources. Organization size should inform practicality; the sensitivity of
the information and applicable obligations should determine required protection.

## Vision

Provide a practical, understandable guide that helps organizations benefit
from ChatGPT, Codex and the OpenAI API while protecting confidential
information and business know-how.

The guide should give business, technical, security and legal participants a
shared basis for deciding what information may be used, under which conditions,
with whose authorization and with what remaining uncertainty.

## Intended value

Readers should be able to distinguish useful AI adoption from unacceptable
exposure of business assets. They should understand which questions to ask
suppliers and internal teams, where organizational rules are needed, and what
must be demonstrated before relying on a safeguard.

The guide should support proportionate decisions: enabling valuable uses where
protection is adequate, identifying additional work where evidence is missing,
and explaining when sensitive information should remain outside a proposed use.

## Scope

The subject is confidentiality and protection of business know-how in
organizational use of ChatGPT, Codex and the OpenAI API. The guide should bring
together business concerns, provider commitments, organizational responsibilities
and the possibilities and limits of technical protection.

The deliverable is an English-language guidance document. Implementation and
validation of a particular organization's deployment are separate activities.
The guide should help readers determine what those activities need to establish.

It is not intended to cover the whole of AI governance. Other questions,
including output quality, fairness, rights in generated material and authority
for consequential actions, require their own assessment. The guide should make
those boundaries clear.

## Success

The guide will be useful if its intended readers can take a proposed OpenAI
use and explain the business benefit, the information at risk, the conditions
needed for acceptable use, the responsible decision makers and the evidence
still required.

Its explanations should be accessible across business and technical roles,
supported by credible sources, and explicit about uncertainty. Readers should
be able to distinguish a proposed safeguard from demonstrated protection and
make an informed decision about their next steps.
