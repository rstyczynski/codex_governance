# Appendix A — ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF and EU AI Act Mapping

This appendix maps the confidentiality analysis to established governance approaches. It connects requirements and risk outcomes to actual chapters, identifies required organizational evidence and records gaps. It is part of the governance analysis, not a claim that the document, an OpenAI subscription or the proposed architecture establishes conformity.

**Assessment date:** 2 November 2026. **Mapping level:** ISO management clauses; selected NIST categories/subcategories; EU provisions screened by role, purpose and application date. The mappings to this document are the author's assessment. “Partial” means relevant draft text exists; it does not mean an organizational control operates. “Gap” means the required process or record is absent. “Conditional” requires a recorded applicability decision.

## A.1 Three approaches, three decisions

| Approach and baseline | When to use it | Decision supported by this document |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ISO/IEC 42001:2023, edition 1](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001) | When the organization adopts an AI management system, including where certification is an objective | Define the confidentiality domain within the organization's management-system scope and identify supporting records |
| [NIST AI RMF 1.0, January 2023](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) | Voluntary risk-management structure for the use case | Connect governance, context, assessment and treatment; the four functions are iterative, not sequential approval stages |
| [EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, consolidated 27 July 2026](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727) | Where territorial scope, operator role and the intended use engage particular legal duties | Determine which duties apply before mapping supporting confidentiality controls; use the Commission's current article texts linked below |

These approaches are complementary. ISO alignment does not itself establish legal compliance; a NIST mapping does not confer certification; an EU provision should not be marked applicable merely because a chapter addresses a similar topic.

## A.4 EU AI Act — applicability before obligations

**Start with the actual activity.** A company using an existing system for its work should assess its deployer role; building or offering an AI application can introduce a provider role. Buying API access or installing a gateway does not settle that classification. Record territorial scope, role, intended purpose, affected people and system changes using [Articles 2 and 3](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-2) and [Article 25](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-25).

The scientific-research exclusions in Article 2 require a separate assessment. Using a general-purpose service to assist confidential research is not, by itself, evidence that an exclusion applies. Nor is confidential content itself a high-risk classification: examine the system's intended purpose under [Article 6 and its annex references](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-6).

| Provision | Applicability question | Document contribution | Record or gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Articles 2–3](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-3) — scope/role | Is there an EU nexus, and is the company a deployer, provider or another operator? | [Governance record](../../chapters/en/04-governance.md) | Conditional: legal applicability and role determination must be recorded per use case |

| [Article 6](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-6) — high-risk classification | Does the intended use meet the product or Annex III tests, including applicable qualifications? | [Use-case decision](../../chapters/en/04-governance.md) | Conditional: supplier classification and company assessment are inputs; employment-related use requires careful screening |
| [Article 25](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-25) — role changes | Do branding, substantial modification or a changed intended purpose engage provider duties for a high-risk system? | [Change triggers](../../chapters/en/04-governance.md), [architecture boundary](../../chapters/en/11-litellm-architecture.md) | Conditional: assess before extending an application or agent; do not assume all changes trigger the same role |
| [Article 26](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-26) — high-risk deployers | Is this a covered high-risk deployment and are the relevant provisions applicable yet? | [Owners](../../chapters/en/04-governance.md), [agent limits](../../chapters/en/13-dlp-and-agent-controls.md), [operations](../../chapters/en/14-validation-and-incident-response.md) | Partial/conditional: instructions, human oversight, relevant inputs, monitoring, notifications and controlled logs need a dedicated record; no automatic blanket duty for every ChatGPT user |
| [Article 27](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-27) — fundamental-rights assessment | Does the deployer and high-risk use fall within the specified categories? | [Impact handoff](../../chapters/en/04-governance.md#43-related-risks-and-approval-handoffs) | Gap if applicable: business confidentiality review is not the required assessment; do not apply it to every private company automatically |
| [Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50) — transparency | Does the company provide direct AI interaction, generate covered content or deploy a specified system? | [Output/action handoffs](../../chapters/en/04-governance.md), [rules](../../chapters/en/06-company-rules.md) | Gap where applicable: identify the exact provider/deployer paragraph, exceptions and disclosure/marking arrangements |

**Additional role-specific duties:** A company becoming a provider of a high-risk system must assess the wider provider requirements, not rely on the deployer row. GPAI model-provider duties are also a separate role assessment; using OpenAI models is not evidence that the customer is a GPAI model provider. The [Commission's Act overview](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) distinguishes these regulatory areas. Provider obligations beyond this confidentiality guide must be assigned to the wider governance process.

**Timing, as checked on 2 November 2026:** The Commission's [current implementation timeline](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/eu-ai-act-implementation-timeline) reflects amendments: general provisions/literacy and original prohibitions from 2 February 2025; GPAI rules from 2 August 2025; transparency generally from 2 August 2026. It lists specified new prohibitions and a content-marking transition for 2 December 2026, Annex III high-risk rules for 2 December 2027, and relevant product-related rules for 2 August 2028. Record applicable transitional provisions; do not substitute the original 2024 timetable or assume all duties apply on one date.

## A.5 Maintain one applicability and evidence register

Add these fields to the Chapter 4 decision record: framework/version or legal article; applicability rationale; role and purpose; effective date/transition; linked document section; required evidence and its location; remaining gap; accountable owner; review date. A reasoned “not applicable” needs a recorded basis and a trigger for reassessment. “Unknown” is an unresolved decision, not an exclusion.

This register connects the document to the wider management system without adding a deployment project. The current appendix completes the document mapping; organizational approval, operating records, full ISO control selection and any legal conclusion remain separate evidence items. Standards alignment does not replace contractual confidentiality or the assessment of residual provider trust.
